Abstract: In this paper, we introduce the Dynamic Hand Gesture 
Dataset (DHGD) tailored for skeleton-based gesture recognition. 
This novel dataset encompasses nine distinct hand gestures, 
each performed by twenty participants, designed to facilitate 
intuitive manipulation of images, including actions such as 
rotations and scaling. Each gesture sequence ia captured over 
70 frames using an RGB-D camera system. Key points of the 
hand are detected in each frame utilizing MediaPipe Hands, 
with key point represented by its three-dimensional coordinates. 
Additionally, we evaluate the recognition accuracy of the nine 
distinct dynamic hand gestures using Spatial-Temporal Graph 
Convolutional Network (ST-GCN). This network is selected as 
a benchmark for graph convolutional networks to validate the 
dataset’s efficacy.  
 
Ⅰ. INTRODUCTION 
Hand gesture recognition has emerged as a pivotal touchless 
user interface, gaining widespread adoption in gaming, 
entertainment, and medical applications, as evidenced by our 
touchless visualization system [1]. Recent advancements have 
leveraged multimodal data, such as color and depth images [2-
7], bolstering the precision of gesture recognition system. 
While static gesture recognition has attained commendable 
accuracy, it fails to capture the subtle dynamics of human 
interactions, consequently restricting its utility in real-time 
and dynamic contexts. To address this deficiency, dynamic 
gesture recognition has become imperative, offering a more 
authentic and intuitive user experience by incorporating 
temporal dynamics and the fluidity of natural motion 
overlooked by static models. In this paper, we have developed 
DHGD, a novel dataset for dynamic hand gesture recognition 
utilizing skeleton-based approaches and deep learning. The 
primary benefits of this dataset are outlined as follows: 
 
A diverse collection of nine distinct hand gestures, 
demonstrated by a group of thirty participants, resulting 
in a rich and diversified dataset 
 
The dynamic hand gestures are specifically designed for 
natural and intuitive interaction with visual interfaces. 
 
A benchmark analysis employing the Spatial-Temporal 
Graph Convolutional Network (ST-GCN) has been 
executed on the dataset. Detailed recognition accuracies 
for each unique gesture are provided, which assist users 
in identifying the most effective dynamic gestures for 
their applications. 
  The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section 
2 describes the dataset's unique attributes in detail. Section 3 
discussed the evaluation of the dataset against benchmark 
performance metrics. Finally, Section 4 presents our 
concluding observation and implications of the research 
findings.

B. 
Hand Image Pre-processing 
  The hand color images were synthesized from the skeleton 
tracking and depth data provided by the Kinect system. The 
initial step involved capturing raw color images of the 
participant's hand, as depicted in Figure 2(a). A meticulous 
calibration procedure was then implemented to ensure precise 
alignment between the color and depth sensors, with a 
particular focus on the articulation points of the right hand. 
For targeted analysis, we demarcated a 300 × 300 pixel square 
as the Region of Interest (ROI), as shown in Figure 2(b). This 
defined ROI allows for a concentrated examination of the 
gesture dynamics within a consistent frame of reference.
  Upon completion of the data collection phase, we utilized 
MediaPipe Hands [9] for the detection of joint points. The 
output data includes coordinates of hand landmarks in both 
image and world reference frames, as well as the classification 
of hand orientation, distinguishing between left and right 
hands. Figure 2(c) exemplifies the imagery post-application of 
MediaPipe Hands, and Figure 2(d) exhibits the reconstructed 
3D visualization derived from the output.

C. 
Dataset Characteristics  
This dataset comprises nine distinct hand gestures, each 
captured in a sequence of 70 frames to comprise a complete 
motion sequence. Recorded from thirty participants, every 
individual performed each gesture five times, culminating in 
a comprehensive dataset of 900 instances (30 participants × 9 
gestures × 5 repetitions). The resolution for each hand gesture 
image is standardized at 300 by 300 pixels, ensuring 
uniformity in the visual data. Significantly, the dataset 
includes nine intuitive hand gestures that mimic common 
rotational and scaling action. Figures 3 (a to h) illustrate the 
diverse range of gestures included in the dataset. Gestures (a 
to c) are predicated on rotational dynamics, with the thumb 
and index finger articulating around the x, y, and z axes, 
respectively. Gesture (d) involves the elevation of the index 
finger followed by a downward motion, mimicking a pressing 
action. Gesture 3(e) is characterized by displaying four fingers 
and then drawing them inward while concurrently tilting the 
palm sideways. In Gesture 3(f), the motion starts with fingers 
directed downwards and culminates in their upward elevation. 
Conversely, Gesture 3(g) depicts the inverse action, where 
fingers begin elevated and are subsequently lowered. Gestures 
(h) and (i) represent the actions of expansion and contraction, 
respectively, where the deliberate spreading and narrowing of 
the thumb and index finger exemplify these concepts.

The Spatial-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (ST-
GCN) [10] is a model introduced in 2018 to detect human 
actions using skeletal data. This model comprises multiple 
layers of spatial and temporal graph convolutions, aimed at 
learning spatial and temporal graphs from the input data. ST-
GCN initiates with spatial convolution, extending the GCN 
model into the temporal domain. Subsequently, convolution 
occurs across frames, advancing in the temporal direction 
through iterative application. 
By employing convolution in both temporal and spatial 
dimensions, ST-GCN effectively captures temporal sequence 
information. These elements are denoted as ST-GCN Units. The 
architecture encompasses six layers of ST-GCN units, 
subsequently followed by a Global Average Pooling (GAP) 
layer and a Softmax classifier. The configuration of the ST-
GCN model employed for hand gesture recognition is 
illustrated in Figure 4. 
 
B. Evaluation Results 
To ensure robust validation, a eight-fold cross-validation 
approach was employed. In this scheme, data from 21 subjects 
were designated for training, while the data from 9 subjects were 
reserved for testing, resulting in a total of 945 training samples 
and 405 test samples encompassing all nine classes. This 
validation process was reiterated times, and the outcomes 
consistently affirmed in each iteration. The average classification 
accuracy achieved using ST-GCN is detailed in Table 1, 
summarizing the recognition accuracy for each gesture. 
Additionally, we experimented by randomly translating 
coordinates for the training data at each epoch and applying data 
augmentation (DA). While the recognition accuracy for rotation 
and zooming in and out appears relatively lower compared to 
other gestures, this discrepancy can be attributed to significant 
individual variations. Nonetheless, the overall recognition 
accuracy for the test set reached 92.41%.  
 
Furthermore, applying data augmentation to the test data resulted 
in a 1.94% improvement in accuracy, achieving a recognition

Ⅳ. CONCLUSION 
In this paper, we presented a dynamic hand gesture dataset for 
skeleton-based gesture recognition based on Kinect sensor and 
MediaPipe Hands. A comprehensive collection of 9 dynamic 
hand gestures was captured from 30 participants and 
subsequently subjected to meticulous preprocessing to extract 
well-segmented hand region images. The ST-GCN model was 
employed as a benchmark for this dataset, demonstrating an 
accuracy of 94.35%. Future efforts will be dedicated to further 
enhancing the accuracy of dynamic hand gesture recognition, 
with the overarching objective of fully unleashing the potential 
of the Skeleton Graph Dynamic Hand Gesture Datasets.
