ABSTRACT
Network security is becoming a critical issue due to the spread
of computer networks to ordinary people. User authentication is
one of the most important part for network security. Although
passwords are used in many personal computers, it has many well-
known problems and it is antiquated. On the other hand, fingerprint
or face based authentication has the risk of information leakage by
reproducing fingerprint or face from the photograph. In this paper,
we have designed a hand gesture-based user authentication using
leap motion to overcome of information leakage problem. We have
collected data from 25 participants and used the data to analyse the
accuracy of our authentication system. The experimental results
are also measured by FAR (False Acceptance Rate) and FRR (False
Rejection Rate). At first, Feature selection has been used to select
important feature out of 246 features. Finally, Dynamic Time Warp-
ing (DTW) based K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN) Classifier has been
used to authenticate a person. Our system has produced 96.73%
accuracy in user authentication. It is also showed 1.25% FAR and
3.62% FRR. Our results indicate that proposed hand gesture with
Leap Motion can be viable authentication approach.

INTRODUCTION
User authentication is a critically important task in cybersecurity.
Nowadays, password and biometrics are generally used for authen-
tication. In particular, notwithstanding text-based passwords are
a traditional method, we suffer from many well-known problems
[1]. The strong passwords are difficult to remember because of the
complex structure. Moreover, we have not to reused passwords. We
don’t prevent leak passwords from the company by cracking. On
the other hand, fingerprints identification or face recognition are
widely used biometrics on mobile phones. Although fingerprints
and face are unique, the attacker can create bogus fingerprints or
face by the photograph of your finger or face. As a result, the at-
tacker can do those recognition methods instead of you. Also, it is
important to notice that fingerprint recognition method necessary
touching to the device.
This research set out to resolve those problems using hand ges-
ture identification. This way has a low risk of leaking information
because we reveal a person’s characteristic from the hand gesture.
In addition, this method does not require touching to the device
owing to do hand gesture in mid-air. This type of authentication is
based upon moving patterns of hand detected in an individual, mak-
ing authentication not only easier but something that can be done
without even having to think about it. We implemented the method
by using a 3D motion sensor called the Leap Motion controller.
The Leap Motion sensor captures user’s hand actions above the
camera and translate them into 3D input using the two infrared
cameras and the infrared LED within the sensor [2]. Recently, there
has been research on camera-based methods using the Leap Motion
controllers. Satoru et al. [3] has used seven types of 3D gestures to
do user authentication. For all the gestures, they have got the true
acceptance rates more than 90 %, and the equal error rates less than
4%. Chan et al. [4] used a circle-drawing gesture captured by leap
motion with one finger for biometric authentication. Saritha et al.
[5] also developed the Leap Motion controller based authentication
system to treat circle-drawing, swipe, screen-tap, and key-tap ges-
tures. 3D hand gesture signatures based system has been developed
by Xiao et al. [6] using the Leap Motion controller. Wong and Kang
[7] implemented a biometric authentication method by using the
Leap Motion controller. They used stationary hand gestures that
consists of free motions of fingers without the motions of hands,
wrists, and arms. Chan et al. [4], Saritha et al. [5], and Xiao et al.
[6] used relatively simple hand gesture because they did not apply
gestures with the complex motions of fingers. Although Wong and
Kang [7] used more complex stationary hand gesture, they still did
not use the motions of hands.
Normally, the Leap Motion controllers based system share the
same motivation as our method. However, our method is more

3.1
Measurement Device
We have measured the hand gesture using Leap Motion. Leap Mo-
tion is a device that captures movements of hands by infrared
cameras. In recently, Leap Motion is in demand as a controller in
VR. Figure 2 shows the recognition of Leap Motion; the hand is
displayed as a skeleton. Basically, we can get the data of the points
of the hand using API of the Leap Motion Controller (Leap Motion
software). Leap Motion is a high-performance device, according to
the official, Leap Motion tracking accuracy is 0.01 mm. whereas,
according to previous research, the accuracy is 0.2 mm for normal
accuracy and 1.2 mm for the dynamic gesture [8]. Leap Motion
photographs hands and fingers lit up by the infrared LEDs by two
monochromatic IR cameras and sends about 120 frames per second
to the host computer. It internally has a standard model of human

3.3
Data Collection Procedure
We measured hand gesture against 25 subjects (10 to 20 years old).
The measurement is the right hand irrespective of dominant hand.
We made some rules for measuring gestures. The description of
the rule are as follows: (i) The position of hand as directly above
Leap Motion; (ii) Keep 15cm or more and less than 40cm from Leap
Motion; (iii) The measurement starts after opening the hand; (iv)
Open hands after measurement ends; (v) Subjects perform hand
gesture naturally; (vi) Measure hand gesture 20 times. Leap Motion’s
camera is facing directly above. As a result, we should consider
about gestures that all fingers do not hide from cameras. As a result,
we determine the hand gesture that taps the fingers with each finger.
We expect that it is easy to detect the feature because all the fingers
are moving.
3.4
Dataset Formation
In our data collection process, 25 participants are involved. Each
participants or person gave hand gesture 20 times. As a result, the
final dataset contained 500 gesture samples.

4.2
Normalizing Hand Gesture Data
In our work, we have used the x, y, z coordinate position of finger
tips, bone’s prejoint, wrist and palm location. Normally, every time
a user cannot do his hand movement by keeping the hand always in
the same location. We may get different data for x, y, z coordinate
position of finger tips, bone’s prejoint, wrist and palm location for
the same user. To overcome this problem, we have normalized our
data (only x, y, z coordinate position) based on the position of the
palm by performing transformations; i.e., moving the palm to the
origin.
5
AUTHENTICATION USING DTW BASED
KNN
5.1
K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN) Classifier
The K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN) algorithm is applied to authenticate
the unknown person in our system. The KNN algorithm make a
decision based on the idea that if majority of the K nearest neighbors
of an unknown data are belong to a certain category, then the
unknown data will be classified into that category [9].
KNN algorithm uses Euclidean distance most frequently as dis-
tance measurement method. However, two sequences must have
equal length of values to calculate the Euclidian distance. At the
same time, the values of the sequences must be one-to-one. Ob-
viously, this distance measurement method does not apply to our
research, because it is not possible to calculate distance between
two the sensor data with variable length. We have used the DTW
(Dynamic Time Warping) algorithm as the distance method to over-
come this limitation.
The calculated DTW distance will be used in the classification or
authentication process of KNN algorithm. Normally, we consider
the value of K is equal to one. In our system, we have tuned the
value of K. The steps of DTW-1NN algorithm for authentication is
given below:
i. Compute the DTW distance between all labeled samples and
the unknown sample.
ii. All computed DTW distances are sorted and the labeled
sample with the smallest distance is taken as the nearest
neighbor of the unknown sample.
iii. Set class label of the nearest neighbor is the class label of the
unknown sample.

FEATURE SELECTION
Feature selection is the method to reduce the number of irrelevant
input features for developing a predictive model. Normally, it re-
duces the number of input variables to decrease the computational
time of the model [11] and to increase the performance of the model
in some cases [12]. In our solution, we have produced individual
feature importance depending on accuracy using the following
algorithm:
i. Select one feature from 246 features.
ii. Find identification accuracy using DTW based KNN for this
feature.
iii. Repeat steps i and ii for all (246) features
iv. Sort those feature according to accuracy
v. Select those features which produce more than 80% accuracy.
7
PERFORMANCE EVALUATION METRICS
In the testing procedure, we have chosen accuracy to assess the
classification performance of KNN as our performance metrics.
Moreover, we have also calculated another two metrics: FAR (False
Acceptance Rate) and FRR (False Rejection Rate) to measure authen-
tication performance of our system. FAR is the rate of accepting
an imposter biometric sample as a legitimate user. On the other
hand, FRR is the rate of rejecting a valid user incorrectly as if the
user is an imposter. In an authentication system, larger FAR value
is more harmful compared to FRR values, because FAR allows im-
poster to easily access the system. For the authentication system,
we expect that it can effectively stop the attack of impostors, and at
the same time, it can give easy access to the legitimate user. So, the
smaller values of FAR and FRR represent the better authentication
performance of the system.
Our final classifier is a multi-class classifier and it gives a label
that represents the predicted name of a user. The general strategy
in evaluating the FAR and FRR errors for multi-class classifiers is to
reduce the problem of multiclass classification to multiple binary
classifications in which each class has its own value of FAR and
FRR errors. In our case, we have calculated the average FAR and
FRR from all classes.

CONCLUSION
Cash cards, password or biometrics information based user authen-
tication systems is vulnerable to external attacks. To improve the
security, a touch-less authentication system has been proposed
in this work. This paper has presented the design and evaluation
hand gesture-based authentication system using the leap motion
sensors. As shown in the experimental results, the system designed
in our study can produce accuracy 96.73% with average FAR 1.25%
and FRR 3.62%. Our findings indicate that proposed hand gesture-
based user authentication can provide a significant enhancement
for traditional authentication systems.
